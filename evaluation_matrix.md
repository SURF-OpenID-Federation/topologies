| Dimension                     | What to evaluate                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Trust model**               | How trust is established between entities: single/multiple Trust Anchors, hierarchical, peer-to-peer, bilateral, or combinations. Assess whether the topology accurately represents the intended trust relationships and whether entities can participate in multiple trust domains. |
| **Governance**                | Who has authority to onboard/offboard entities, define policies, delegate authority, and change federation rules. Assess how centralized or distributed governance is and whether different domains can retain autonomy.                                                             |
| **Scalability**               | How the topology behaves as the number of entities, federations, trust relationships, and Trust Chains increases. Consider chain length, number of statements, resolution traffic, and whether adding entities increases complexity linearly or disproportionately.                  |
| **Availability**              | Identify dependencies on Trust Anchors, Intermediate entities, federation endpoints, DNS, etc. Assess whether temporary unavailability prevents trust decisions and how effectively caching or previously retrieved statements can mitigate failures.                                |
| **Security**                  | Assess attack surface, isolation between entities, key compromise, trust-chain attacks, and recovery mechanisms. An important metric is the **blast radius** of compromising a Trust Anchor or Intermediate.      
| **Key rotation**                      | Evaluate the impact of key rotation by Entities in the topology                                  |
| **Policy**                    | Evaluate how security and operational policies are expressed, inherited, constrained, and combined. Consider whether policies can be applied at different federation levels and how conflicts or incompatible policies are handled.                                                  |
| **Protocol support**          | Assess how naturally the topology supports OIDC, OAuth, OpenID4VCI and OpenID4VP. In particular, consider whether the same entity and trust infrastructure can support multiple roles and protocols without duplicating federation structures.                                       |
| **Federation composition**    | Evaluate whether federations can be nested, interconnected, or combined into federations-of-federations. Consider cross-federation trust, policy propagation, multiple federation memberships, and how trust boundaries are maintained.                                              |
| **Operations**                | Assess the complexity of onboarding, offboarding, key rotation, policy changes, entity migration, and federation restructuring. Also consider how much manual coordination is required between organizations.                                                                        |
| **Auditability**              | Determine whether a trust decision can be explained and reproduced: *Why did A trust B at time T?* Evaluate the ability to inspect Trust Chains, policies, Trust Marks, keys, and historical changes.                                                                                |
| **Privacy**                   | Assess what information about entities, relationships, roles, and organizational structure is exposed. Consider whether the topology creates unnecessary metadata disclosure or enables parties to infer relationships they do not need to know.                                     |
| **Performance**               | Evaluate latency, bandwidth, computational cost, and number of federation-resolution operations required to establish trust. Pay particular attention to the impact of Trust Chain depth and metadata retrieval.                                                                     |
| **Resilience**                | Assess behaviour during partial failures, network partitions, stale metadata, compromised components, and federation splits. Consider whether the system can continue operating safely in a degraded or disconnected state.                                                          |
| **Migration**                 | Evaluate how easily entities, intermediates, Trust Anchors, or entire federations can be moved or reorganized. This is important because the organizational topology will likely change over the federation's lifetime.                                                              |
| **Legal / regulatory**        | Assess how responsibility and liability are distributed between Trust Anchors, intermediates, issuers, verifiers, OPs, etc. Consider jurisdiction, accreditation requirements, data protection, and whether governance responsibilities are clearly attributable.                    |
| **Implementation complexity** | Evaluate the complexity of implementing, deploying, securing, and operating the topology. Include number of federation components, required protocols, key-management infrastructure, software dependencies, and operational expertise.                                              |
| **OpenID Federation Capabilities** | Evaluate the topology while explicitly taking into account core capabilities of the OpenID Federation protocol, including: Trust Chain resolution, ability to use trustmarks , use of resolver endpoint                                              |
